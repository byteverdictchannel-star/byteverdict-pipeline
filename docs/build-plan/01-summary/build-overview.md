# Build Plan — Trending Clips Channel (Zero Budget)

**Read this first.** This is the plan in one pass. The L2 files beneath it hold the detail for each dimension.

---

## The Channel

A trending clips channel that:
- Clips existing third-party war/news footage (broadcast, agency wire, other creators)
- Publishes cut + text overlay clips (headlines, captions, context lines, timestamps)
- Targets TikTok → YouTube Shorts → Instagram Reels, accounts already live
- Launches with a small empirical test batch (a handful over 1–2 weeks)
- Has monetization prioritized ASAP

## The Constraint

Zero budget means zero licensing money. The channel cannot clear rights through licensing for the bulk of its content. Every clip's defensibility rests on a combination of source selection, transformation arguments (text overlays), and platform tolerance — none of which is individually conclusive. This is the honest shape of the model.

## The Binding Constraints (in order of immediacy)

1. **Platform sensitivity enforcement is the fastest failure mode.** A clip can have a plausible fair-use posture and still be removed, age-gated, or throttled for graphic war content. Screen for this first.
2. **Reused-content patterns are the channel-level risk.** Individual clips being live ≠ channel being safe or monetizable. The aggregate content pattern matters as much as any single clip.
3. **Copyright claims are most likely on YouTube.** Content ID and takedown infrastructure are most mature there. Source selection (avoiding wire/broadcast with known enforcement posture) is the primary defense.
4. **Monetization eligibility is a separate gate from publishing.** A clip live on all three platforms can still be throttled, age-gated, or excluded from monetization programs on each.
5. **Attribution is good practice, not a shield.** Credit your sources, but don't treat a credit line as legal or platform clearance.

## The Build in Layers

### Layer 1 — Sourcing
Free footage is everywhere. The skill is finding the right footage and knowing what you're looking at.
- **Tier 1 (relatively lower risk):** Public domain / government footage where jurisdiction actually puts it in PD; explicitly permissively licensed footage. Verify per source — not a blanket safe harbor.
- **Tier 2 (moderate risk):** Major broadcast news clips, wire service footage, established outlets. Most trending clips come from here. Highest scrutiny.
- **Tier 3 (higher risk):** Smaller creators, aggregators, unclear-origin footage. Hard to evaluate, harder to defend.
- **Tier 4 (avoid):** Watermarked stock/wire footage, sources with "do not use" notices, other creators' compilations (double risk).

**Rule:** Don't build the channel on Tier 4. Be deliberate about Tier 2. Variety across sources is a risk-control pattern.

### Layer 2 — Overlay Design (the transformation work)
This is where defensibility lives. The same source footage is more or less defensible depending on what you layer onto it.
- **What overlays should do:** Add context, not just sensationalism. Specific headlines (who/what/when/why), context lines (what happened, who's speaking, what's at stake), timestamps, source attribution.
- **What thin overlays look like:** A headline that restates what's visible, a timestamp, no real added context. Weakest transformative posture.
- **What strong overlays look like:** Specific, accurate, added context that changes what the viewer is getting from the clip — enough that the clip-plus-overlay is arguably a different work than the clip alone.
- **Attribution:** Include it as good practice. It's not a defense, but it's honest and supports trust.

### Layer 3 — Editing & Tooling (free only)
- Cut the clip to a self-contained moment — shorter, selected segments are a stronger position than near-whole reuse.
- Add overlays in the editing tool.
- Export at platform-native aspect ratios and lengths.
- Free tools only (see L2 `03-editing-tooling.md`).

### Layer 4 — Distribution (one source clip → three surfaces)
- TikTok: Shorter, fastest reach, most aggressive reused-content and sensitivity enforcement. Best test surface.
- YouTube Shorts: Long-game monetization anchor (YPP). Different reused-content enforcement. Most likely surface for a concrete copyright claim.
- Instagram Reels: Distribution surface, different audience. Focus on account health and reach; monetization is secondary.

Repurpose the same cut clip to all three with platform-native sizing. Don't post identical files — resize and re-export per platform.

### Layer 5 — Test Batch
A handful of clips over 1–2 weeks. Enough to see platform response, audience behavior, and your own production rhythm — not enough to commit to a cadence you haven't earned.
- Mix source tiers (a few Tier 1 where available, selective Tier 2)
- Vary overlay quality deliberately (some strong, some thin — see what performs)
- Screen every candidate for sensitive content before editing
- Log everything (source, tier, what you did, what happened)

### Layer 6 — Monetization (ASAP intent, realistic timing)
- **YouTube YPP:** Primary long-game anchor. Requires eligibility thresholds + originality/reused-content standing. Earned over time, subject to content-pattern review.
- **TikTok Creator programs:** Separate eligibility gates. Reused-content expectations apply.
- **Instagram monetization features:** Program-specific eligibility.
- **Off-platform ownership:** Newsletter/site/other channel — builds an asset the platforms don't control. Relevant even if monetization is primarily platform-based.
- **Sponsor-readiness:** Structure the channel so it can take sponsorship later (clean branding, audience data, consistent format). War/news sponsors are a narrower category — don't over-index on this early.

**Honest framing:** Monetization ASAP means planning for it now, not expecting it immediately. Platform monetization eligibility is earned, not automatic.

## The Labor Model (zero budget, solo)

Right now it's you and free tools and subagent help where it fits. The labor sits in:
- **Sourcing:** Finding, vetting, logging candidates. Runnable as a daily hunt.
- **Overlay writing:** The real value-add. Writing specific, accurate, context-adding overlays is skilled work.
- **Editing:** Cutting + overlaying + exporting. Can be templated and batched.
- **Screening:** Sensitive content checks before editing. Non-negotiable.
- **Distribution:** Re-purposing one clip to three platforms.

As volume increases, the leverage points are: better hunting-ground lists, overlay templates, batch editing, and eventually subagent-assisted sourcing/vetting/overlay drafting.

## Where Subagents Fit (at zero budget)

- **Sourcing research:** Find clips, surface candidates, log them. You vet and decide.
- **Overlay drafting:** Draft overlay text for vetted clips. You edit for accuracy and tone.
- **Trend / competitor research:** Use YouTube Full skill to research what's performing on YouTube Shorts and what competitor channels are doing.
- **Distribution prep:** Format and organize files for multi-platform posting.
- **Not good for (yet):** Final clip decisions, sensitive-content judgment calls, anything that requires outsourcing a legal/compliance decision.

## The First 90 Days (sketch — detail in L2 `06-30-60-90-plan.md`)

- **Days 1–14:** Source hunting-ground list, overlay template, editing tool setup, 5–10 test clips, post to TikTok + Shorts + Reels, log everything, watch platform response.
- **Days 15–30:** Analyze what performed (reach, retention, engagement, platform flags), iterate overlay design and source mix, scale to a cadence that fits your labor, begin YouTube channel development for YPP trajectory.
- **Days 31–90:** Dial in the format that works, increase volume if the labor model supports it, start building toward monetization eligibility (originality, pattern, account health), set up off-platform ownership layer, test monetization eligibility gates as they come into view.

## What Success Looks Like

- A channel that finds a clip format that gets traction on at least one surface
- A sourcing workflow that reliably produces vetted candidates
- Overlay design that adds real value and improves the transformation posture
- A multi-platform distribution habit that doesn't slow you down
- A clear picture of which monetization paths are in reach and when

## What This Plan Does Not Promise

- That any particular clip is legal or safe
- That monetization is immediate
- That platforms won't remove, age-gate, or throttle content
- That "text overlay only, no voiceover" is a strong transformative posture (it's the thinner end)
- That zero budget changes the legal line — it doesn't; it just limits the tools you have to manage risk
