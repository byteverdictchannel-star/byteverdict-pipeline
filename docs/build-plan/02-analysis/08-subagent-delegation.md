# Subagent Delegation Map — What Help Fits, What Doesn't

**At zero budget, subagent help is free labor with boundaries.** The boundaries matter: subagents are good at research, drafting, and prep work. They are not good at final judgment calls, sensitive-content decisions, or anything that outsources a legal/compliance decision.

---

## 1. The Principle

Subagents extend your capacity. They do not replace your judgment. For a clips channel in this model — third-party footage, text overlays, zero budget, monetization ASAP, contested legal posture — the right use of subagents is to handle the labor that can be handed off without handing off the decisions that need to stay with you.

---

## 2. What Subagents Can Do (Useful Delegation)

### Sourcing research

- Find clips, surface candidates, log them.
- Run hunting-ground searches, compile candidate lists, tier them preliminarily.
- You vet and decide. The subagent surfaces; you filter.

### Overlay drafting

- Draft overlay text for vetted clips — headlines, context lines, timestamps, source attribution.
- You edit for accuracy, tone, and whether the overlay actually adds value.
- This is the most natural delegation: drafting is labor; accuracy and value are judgment.

### Trend / competitor research

- Use the YouTube Full skill to research what's performing on YouTube Shorts and what competitor channels are doing.
- Surface trending topics, formats, lengths, and competitor patterns.
- You interpret the research and decide what to test.

### Distribution prep

- Format and organize files for multi-platform posting.
- Prepare per-platform exports, captions, and posting notes.
- You review and post.

### Clip log maintenance

- Help maintain the source log and clip log — entries, tracking, patterns.
- You review and act on the patterns.

### Research generally

- Research sources, outlets, topics, and competitor channels.
- Compile what's findable and free.
- You evaluate and act.

---

## 3. What Subagents Should Not Do (Yet)

### Final clip decisions

- Whether to clip a candidate, which clip to produce, which overlay to use — these are your decisions, not the subagent's.
- The subagent can draft and surface; you decide.

### Sensitive-content judgment calls

- Deciding whether a clip's graphic content will trip platform flags is a judgment call that should stay with you, possibly with your own viewing.
- A subagent can flag obvious candidates; it should not be the final sensitive-content screen.

### Legal / compliance decisions

- Nothing that outsources a legal or compliance decision to a subagent.
- The legal report is a landscape and risk assessment; it is not a clearance, and it is not something to delegate to a subagent as if it were legal advice.
- If a clip raises a real legal question, that's a question for you to think about carefully, not to hand off.

### Posting / publishing / purchasing / destructive changes

- Per the autonomy guardrails: posting, publishing, purchasing, and destructive changes need your explicit approval.
- Subagents should not post clips, spend money, or make irreversible changes without you.

---

## 4. The Handoff Pattern

For each delegation:

1. **You define the task and the boundaries.** What the subagent is looking for, what it should produce, what it should not decide.
2. **The subagent produces.** A candidate list, a draft overlay, a research summary, a prep file.
3. **You review and decide.** Vet the candidates, edit the overlays, interpret the research, approve the post.
4. **You log.** Whatever the subagent produced gets folded into the source log or clip log, with your decisions recorded.

The subagent is a force multiplier on labor, not a replacement for your judgment or your account.

---

## 5. Where Delegation Fits in the 90-Day Arc

### Days 1–14 (test batch)

- Minimal delegation. You're establishing the workflow; the test batch is small.
- Possible: sourcing research to build the hunting-ground list and candidate backlog; overlay drafting for vetted clips.
- You do the production, the screening, the posting, and the logging.

### Days 15–30 (iterate and start scaling)

- More delegation as volume grows: sourcing research, overlay drafting, trend/competitor research.
- You still do the judgment calls and the posting.
- Begin to see where subagent help saves real time vs. where it's just another step.

### Days 31–90 (build toward scale and monetization)

- Delegation becomes more useful as volume increases: batch sourcing, overlay drafting at volume, distribution prep, competitor monitoring.
- The leverage points: better hunting-ground lists, overlay templates, batch editing, and subagent-assisted sourcing/vetting/overlay drafting.
- You retain the judgment calls, the sensitive-content screen, and the account-level decisions.

---

## 6. What Delegation Does Not Solve

- **The legal posture.** Subagents don't make clips legal or safe.
- **The platform risk.** Subagents don't override platform sensitivity or reused-content rules.
- **The monetization timing.** Subagents don't make YPP eligibility automatic.
- **The judgment.** The channel's character — what it clips, how it overlays, what it posts — is shaped by your decisions, not by what a subagent can draft.

---

## 7. Honest Limits

- **Subagent help is useful labor, not a cure.** It can extend capacity; it can't make a bad source good or a thin overlay defensible.
- **Subagent drafting still needs your editing.** A drafted overlay is a draft, not a finished product. Accuracy and value are your call.
- **Don't delegate what shouldn't be delegated.** If a task is a judgment call, a sensitivity call, or a compliance call, it stays with you.
