## Candidate 1 — War/Development: Iran War — "Trump: Iran 'Completely Collapsing'"

### Topic
The US-Israel war on Iran, specifically Trump's public claim that Iran is "completely collapsing," and the broader state of the conflict as of August 24, 2026 — economic pressure, sanctions, strikes, regional fallout.

### Why this candidate
- **Current.** Multiple sources today (Aug 24): Al Jazeera liveblog, Britannica 2026 Iran war explainer, CNN live updates, Reuters, Guardian. The story is live and developing.
- **Specific claim by a named figure.** "Iran completely collapsing" is a quotable, attributable statement — stronger than a vague "tensions rising." A clip can lead with the claim and add context about what's actually happening (economic collapse indicators, sanctions, strikes, regional displacement).
- **High reach potential.** War/conflict content gets reach when it's specific and current. This is specific and current.
- **High sensitivity risk.** War footage — combat, destruction, casualties — can be graphic. This candidate carries the channel's fastest failure mode. The screen below is mandatory.

### Overlay skeleton (Tier A)

**Headline (one line, top):**
> Trump: Iran is "completely collapsing"

**Context lines (2–3 lines, beneath headline, readable):**
> - Trump's claim comes as the US prepares more sanctions and Iran's rial hits a record low.
> - The conflict has displaced millions across the region, including more than one-sixth of Lebanon's population.
> - Iran has warned neighbors against joining the US economic war.

**Timestamp (small, discrete):**
> Aug 24, 2026

**Source attribution (bottom, small, accurate):**
> Source: footage via [OUTLET] — see source log

**What the overlay does:**
- Leads with the attributable claim (specific, verifiable)
- Adds context the raw clip doesn't give (economic indicators, displacement, the warning to neighbors)
- Attributes the source (good-faith practice, not a legal defense)
- The clip itself should be a news-report or wire clip that can be attributed and that doesn't gratuitously show graphic violence

### Source target
- **Tier A (preferred):** Reuters, AP, AFP wire footage of the Iran-related developments — economic, diplomatic, or measured military coverage. Reuters' "Alibaba launches Wan3.0..." entry today shows Reuters is active and current; the same wire is a good target for Iran coverage.
- **Tier B (acceptable):** Major broadcaster news clips (BBC, CNN, Al Jazeera) that cover the Iran developments — but check the sensitivity of the specific footage before using.
- **Tier C (use with caution):** Other creators' Iran clips — verify they didn't source from a wire/broadcaster you're avoiding.
- **Tier D (avoid unless unavoidable):** Unattributed social clips of combat/casualties — high sensitivity and high source-uncertainty.

### Sensitive-content screen (MANDATORY before posting)

Run through every item. If any item is a "yes" that can't be resolved by choosing different footage, the clip is not cleared for this surface in its current form — either find less graphic footage of the same development, or drop the candidate.

1. **Does the footage show death, injury, or graphic violence?** If yes, can you find coverage of the same development that conveys the story without graphic imagery? If you can't, the clip fails this screen.
2. **Does the footage show destroyed infrastructure, burning, or aftermath that is graphic?** Same test — is there a less graphic way to convey the same development?
3. **Is the footage from a source whose broadcast package includes graphic warnings or is itself graphic?** A wire/broadcaster clip may be newsworthy but still too graphic for short-form. Check the specific clip, not the outlet's general reputation.
4. **Does the clip's first frame or most prominent shot trigger a pause?** If the first thing a viewer sees is graphic, the platform's automated systems and human reviewers are more likely to act. Test the opening frame specifically.
5. **If the clip is war footage, is the "news value" in the graphic content or in the development?** If the value is in the development (the claim, the sanctions, the displacement), the graphic content is not necessary. Prefer footage that conveys the development without the graphic content.

**Decision rule:** If you can't find footage of this development that passes the screen, do not post a graphic version. The candidate may still be worth covering with footage that conveys the development without graphic content — or it may need to wait for a less sensitive angle.

### Platform-specific notes for this candidate
- **TikTok:** Aggressive on sensitive/graphic content. War footage is the highest-risk surface here. Screen hardest for TikTok.
- **YouTube Shorts:** YPP monetization has advertiser-friendly content considerations; graphic war content can be age-restricted or demonetized. Screen for monetization exposure, not just takedown.
- **Instagram Reels:** Distribution and monetization paths differ; graphic content can be removed or deprioritized. Screen for reach impact.

## Batch-Level Notes

### Topic mix rationale
- **One clip per lane.** This batch samples the three genre areas the brief names (war/development, tech, viral/trending). The goal is to see which lane gets traction, which gets flagged, and which gets clean reach — not to maximize any one clip.
- **Tier A overlays on all three.** The first pass should use the best overlay craft available, so the data reflects the format's best case, not a weak case. Later batches can deliberately vary overlay depth (Tier B) to test whether overlay quality correlates with performance.

### Sensitive-content asymmetry across the batch
- **Candidate 1 (Iran war) is the high-sensitivity clip.** It's the only one that carries the channel's fastest failure mode. The screen above is mandatory and is the binding pre-post check.
- **Candidates 2 and 3 are expected-clean.** They still get a standard screen (check the specific footage for anything unexpected, check the overlay for accuracy), but they are not in the war-content risk zone.
- **The batch's data value includes the sensitivity signal.** If Candidate 1 gets flagged/age-gated/throttled and Candidates 2 and 3 don't, that's a real signal about the genre-mix trade-off. Record it.

### Source-lineage discipline across the batch
- **All three candidates have a Tier A source target.** Reuters (C1, C2), CNN/Reuters (C3). The goal is to source from Tier 1 wire/broadcast where possible, and to log the source lineage accurately.
- **If a Tier A source isn't available for a candidate**, fall back to Tier B (major broadcaster) and log the source. Do not default to Tier D (unattributed social clips) without logging why.
- **Log the source lineage for every clip.** Source, portion used, overlay, sensitive-content screen result, platform check, posted date/time, response. The clip log is the binding record.

### Posting order
- **Post Candidate 2 (tech) and Candidate 3 (viral/trending) first** if you want cleaner data before the high-sensitivity clip. That gives you a baseline on the cleaner lanes before you test the war lane.
- **Or post Candidate 1 first** if you want the sensitivity signal immediately. Either order is fine; the choice is about whether you want the cleaner baseline first or the sensitivity test first.
- **Recommended:** Post Candidate 2 and Candidate 3 first (cleaner baseline), then Candidate 1 (sensitivity test). Watch the platform response on all three before drawing conclusions.

### What "response" means for the first batch
- **TikTok:** Did the clip get reach? Was it flagged, age-gated, or removed? Did it get any monetization eligibility signal?
- **YouTube Shorts:** Did the clip get reach? Was it age-restricted or demonetized? Any copyright claim or Content ID match? Any YPP eligibility signal (not expected this early, but note it)?
- **Instagram Reels:** Did the clip get reach? Was it removed or deprioritized? Any feature-eligibility signal?

### What to do with the data
- **If a clip gets flagged or removed:** Record what happened, which platform, what content triggered it. That's data about the sensitivity boundary. Don't assume it's final — platforms can be inconsistent — but log it.
- **If a clip gets clean reach:** Record the reach, the platform, the overlay, the source. That's data about what's working.
- **If a clip gets no reach:** Record that too. No-reach data is data — it tells you the clip didn't get traction, for whatever reason.
- **Don't over-interpret a batch of three.** Three clips is a starting point, not a conclusion. The batch's job is to generate the first real data points and to surface any immediate problems (sensitivity, sourcing, overlay, platform). The 30/60/90 plan is where the pattern starts to emerge.
