# 30/60/90 Day Plan — Trending Clips Channel (Zero Budget)

**A sketch of the first 90 days, grounded in the test-batch-first approach.** This is a planning scaffold, not a promise. The actual cadence and direction come from what the test batch and early data show.

---

## 1. The Shape of the First 90 Days

- **Days 1–14:** Test batch. Build the workflow, produce a handful of clips, distribute across the three surfaces, log everything, watch platform response.
- **Days 15–30:** Review the test batch, iterate on the format that shows signal, begin scaling the cadence the labor model supports, start building toward YouTube monetization trajectory.
- **Days 31–90:** Dial in the working format, increase volume if the labor model supports it, build toward monetization eligibility, stand up the off-platform ownership layer, and test monetization eligibility gates as they come into view.

The 90-day plan is not "100 clips and monetization." It's "find the format that works, build the workflow that sustains it, and start the monetization trajectory."

---

## 2. Days 1–14 — Test Batch

### Goals

- Establish the hunting-ground list and the source log.
- Set up the overlay template and the editing tool.
- Produce 5–10 test clips across a tight topic slice (war + tech + viral, 2–3 areas).
- Distribute across TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels.
- Log everything: source, tier, overlay, sensitive-content flag, production time, platform response.
- Watch platform response and audience behavior.

### Outcomes we want

- A working workflow from source to posted clip.
- Real per-clip labor numbers.
- First read on which topic areas and which overlay qualities perform.
- First platform flags (good ones if none; informative ones if some).

### What to watch for

- Sensitive-content flags on war clips (the fastest failure mode).
- Reused-content / reach-throttling signals.
- Any copyright claims, especially on YouTube.
- Which overlay quality tier seemed to perform better.
- Which topic area got the most real reach.

### Decision point at Day 14

Review the test batch and decide:
- What format to carry forward.
- What cadence is realistic.
- Whether to broaden the topic slice or stay tight.
- Whether to invest more in overlay craft.

---

## 3. Days 15–30 — Iterate and Start Scaling

### Goals

- Carry forward the format that showed signal.
- Improve the overlay design based on what the test showed.
- Adjust the source mix based on what behaved as expected.
- Scale to a cadence the labor model supports — not an arbitrary cadence.
- Begin YouTube channel development with an eye toward YPP trajectory (originality, pattern, account health).
- Start the off-platform ownership layer (newsletter/site/other channel) — early, not after monetization.

### Outcomes we want

- A clearer picture of the working format.
- A sustainable cadence, even if modest.
- Early momentum on at least one surface.
- The channel moving from "test batch" to "early channel."

### What to watch for

- Whether the channel's aggregate content pattern is moving toward or away from reused-content perception.
- Whether platform flags are repeating on certain source types or content types.
- Whether the labor model is actually sustainable at the chosen cadence.
- Early signs of audience building (return viewers, follows, consistent engagement on a format).

### Decision point at Day 30

Review and decide:
- Is the working format clear enough to scale?
- Is the cadence sustainable?
- Is YouTube trajectory on track, or do we need to adjust the content pattern?
- Do we broaden the topic slice now, or stay focused longer?

---

## 4. Days 31–90 — Build Toward Monetization and Scale

### Goals

- Dial in the format that works and increase volume if the labor model supports it.
- Continue building toward monetization eligibility — originality, pattern, account health, consistency.
- Test monetization eligibility gates as they come into view (YPP, TikTok Creator programs, Instagram monetization features).
- Stand up or grow the off-platform ownership layer.
- Keep sponsor-readiness in mind — clean branding, audience data, consistent format.
- Expand sourcing and overlay workflows where subagent help fits.

### Outcomes we want

- A channel that has found a working format and is building an audience on at least one surface.
- A sustainable production workflow.
- A clear read on which monetization paths are in reach and roughly when.
- An owned audience layer in progress.

### What to watch for

- Monetization eligibility signals — what the platforms say about your standing, reach, originality, and content pattern.
- Whether the channel is trending toward or away from reused-content perception as it scales.
- Whether volume is sustainable or whether the workflow needs more automation/batching/subagent help.
- Whether the off-platform layer is actually building an owned audience.

### Decision point at Day 90

Review and decide:
- What the channel looks like at 90 days vs. the test batch.
- Which monetization paths are in reach.
- What the next 90 days should focus on (scale, monetization, format expansion, off-platform ownership, or a combination).

## 5. The Cadence Question

The cadence is not fixed at the start. It's derived:

- **From the test batch:** What was the real per-clip labor?
- **From the labor model:** What can you sustain solo at zero budget?
- **From the data:** What cadence does the channel need to grow, and what cadence does it can afford?

Possible cadences:
- **Low (a few per week):** Sustainable, careful, quality-focused. Good if the format is strong and labor is the constraint.
- **Moderate (several per week):** Needs templates, batch editing, and a repeatable workflow. Reasonable if the format works and you can sustain it.
- **Higher (daily+):** Needs templated overlays, batch sourcing, and eventually subagent help. Only commit to this if the channel has earned it and the workflow supports it.

The 90-day plan does not commit to a high cadence. It commits to finding the cadence the channel earns.

## 6. The Monetization Trajectory (Not Immediate)

Monetization ASAP means planning for it now, not expecting it on day one.

### Platform monetization

- **YouTube YPP:** The primary long-game anchor. Earned over time; subject to content-pattern review. The channel should be building toward it from the start — originality, pattern, account health — without assuming it's automatic.
- **TikTok Creator programs:** Separate eligibility gates. Reused-content expectations apply. Plan for it; don't assume it.
- **Instagram monetization features:** Program-specific eligibility. Secondary surface per the brief.

### Off-platform ownership

- Newsletter, site, or other channel — an asset you control.
- Relevant even if monetization is primarily platform-based.
- Builds early; pays off when platform dynamics change.

### Sponsor-readiness

- Structure the channel so it can take sponsorship later: clean branding, audience data, consistent format.
- Tech + trending news broadens the long-term sponsor/affiliate surface vs. war-only — relevant, but not immediate.
- Don't over-index on sponsorship early; the audience has to exist first.

## 8. Honest Limits

- **90 days is not a guarantee of monetization.** It's a trajectory. Some channels reach eligibility; some don't on the first pass; some need more time or a different content pattern.
- **90 days is not a guarantee of scale.** There are no guarantees in this model. The plan is to build the conditions for growth, not to promise it.
- **The format could change.** If the test batch shows that a different format works better, the 90-day plan should adapt. It's a scaffold, not a script.
- **The genre mix could narrow or broaden.** If war content consistently gets flagged and tech content performs cleaner, the channel may lean that way. If viral news is the reach play, the channel may lean that way. Let the data inform the mix.
