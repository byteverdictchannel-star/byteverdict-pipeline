## YouTube Monetization / Partner Program — Key Excerpts (verbatim)

*Source: YouTube Help — "How to earn money on YouTube," https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72857. Retrieved 2026-08-24.*

### 1. Partner Program overview

> "You can earn money on YouTube by applying for and being accepted to the YouTube Partner Program. Only channels that follow our YouTube channel monetization policies may be able to monetize."

> "We won't tell you what you can create on YouTube, but we do have a responsibility to do right by our viewers, creators and advertisers. If you're in the YouTube Partner Program, you can earn money through YouTube. When in the YouTube Partner Program, we hold you to a higher standard."

> "To make sure we're rewarding good creators, we review your channel before you're accepted in the YouTube Partner Program. We also constantly review channels to make sure you're meeting all our policies and guidelines."

### 2. Ad revenue eligibility thresholds

> "Ad revenue:
> - 1,000 subscribers
> - Either:
>   - 4,000 qualified watch hours on long form videos in the last 365 days
>   - 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days"

Additional requirements: be at least 18, live in a country where YPP is available, accept the relevant contract Modules, and create content that meets advertiser-friendly content guidelines.

### 3. Per-feature eligibility

The page notes that each monetization feature (channel memberships, shopping, Super Chat/Stickers, Super Thanks, ad revenue, YouTube Premium revenue, shopping from other brands) has its own eligibility requirements on top of subscriber/view thresholds, and that "If our reviewers believe that your channel or video is not eligible, specific features may not be available."

### 4. Advertiser-friendly content guidelines

> "Create content that meets our advertiser-friendly content guidelines." (Referenced as a requirement for ad revenue.)

## YouTube Community Guidelines & Policy Framework — Structural Landscape

*Community Guidelines page (https://www.youtube.com/t/community_guidelines) is referenced by the ToS as part of the Agreement. The following is the structural landscape, not a verbatim capture.*

YouTube's relevant policy frameworks for the channel include:
- **Community Guidelines** — content standards including, at relevant times, violent or graphic content, harmful or dangerous content, and related categories.
- **Copyright enforcement systems** — Content ID, manual takedown/claim flows, and the copyright strike system.
- **Channel monetization policies** — including reused-content and "repetitive content" expectations, and advertiser-friendly content guidelines.
- **Shorts-specific frameworks** — Shorts feed, Shorts monetization, and the way YouTube treats short-form vertical content.

**Implication for the channel:** YouTube's copyright enforcement infrastructure (Content ID, takedowns, strikes) is the most mature of the three platforms, making it the most likely surface for a concrete copyright-claim event. At the same time, YPP monetization eligibility depends on channel-level content pattern and advertiser-friendly content considerations, which are separate from whether any individual video is live.

## Why These Excerpts Matter for the Channel

1. **The ToS restricts use of Content without authorization.** The channel's model — clipping and republishing others' Content — is directly in the zone of "you are not allowed to... reproduce, distribute... [or] otherwise use any part of the Service or any Content except... with prior written permission from YouTube and, if applicable, the respective rights holders." This is a platform-contract prohibition, separate from copyright law.

2. **Content is the uploader's responsibility.** YouTube's ToS places responsibility on the uploader for Content that violates third-party rights or policies. The channel cannot offload this to the platform.

3. **Monetization is eligibility-gated.** YPP requires threshold achievement AND content-policy compliance AND advertiser-friendly content. A clip being live does not mean it is monetizable. Thresholds (e.g., 1,000 subscribers + 10M Shorts views in 90 days for ad revenue) are a concrete hurdle.

4. **YouTube reviews channels for monetization and "constantly reviews channels."** This is the structural basis for the analysis's point that monetization eligibility is an ongoing, pattern-based review, not a one-time approval.

5. **California law and 1-year limitation.** Contractual disputes with YouTube would likely be governed by California law in Santa Clara County courts, with a 1-year limitations period — relevant for ToS-level disputes distinct from copyright.
