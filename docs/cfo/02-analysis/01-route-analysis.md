## Route 1 — YouTube Partner Program (YPP)

### What it is

YouTube's monetization program. Once eligible, the channel earns from ads on its videos, including Shorts. YPP has eligibility thresholds, originality/reused-content standing requirements, account-standing requirements, and advertiser-friendly content considerations.

### Fit with this channel

**Medium-to-high fit, with a significant caveat.**

- **Surface fit:** The channel is already live on YouTube. Shorts is a real distribution surface. YouTube is the most mature monetization infrastructure of the three platforms.
- **Format fit:** This is the caveat. The channel's format — third-party footage with text overlays — is in the zone that YPP's reused-content and originality expectations scrutinize. A channel that largely posts cut clips with thin overlays may fall into the zone YPP targets for denial or removal.
- **Genre fit:** War/news content can be advertiser-sensitive regardless of eligibility. Tech/trending content is generally more advertiser-friendly. The genre mix matters for YPP's advertiser-friendly content considerations.

### Speed to first revenue

**Slow (trajectory, not immediate).**

YPP eligibility is earned over time. The channel must:
- Reach or be on track toward eligibility thresholds (subscribers, watch time / Shorts views — exact numbers and mechanics change over time and by program version).
- Establish an originality/reused-content standing that passes review.
- Maintain account standing and policy compliance.
- Have content that is advertiser-friendly enough to monetize.

None of this is day-one. The channel starts at zero audience and zero monetization history. The realistic path is a trajectory, not an event.

### Sustainability and scale

**High, if eligible.**

If the channel qualifies for YPP and maintains eligibility, YouTube monetization is the most scalable and most direct path among the routes. It can produce revenue at scale from an audience the channel has built. It's the route with the highest upside if the channel clears the bar.

### Downside and risk

- **Eligibility may be blocked by the format.** If the channel's text-overlay-only, third-party-footage model can't establish sufficient originality/reused-content standing, YPP may be out of reach on the current format. This is the central risk.
- **Eligibility may be blocked by content pattern.** A channel that posts mostly war footage with graphic content may face advertiser-friendly content issues independent of eligibility.
- **Copyright claims are most likely on YouTube.** Content ID and takedown infrastructure are most mature here. A claim can affect monetization, and strikes can threaten the channel.
- **Platform dependency.** YouTube controls the monetization. Changes to rules, thresholds, or enforcement can affect the channel's revenue.

### What it requires from the operator

- Build toward sufficient originality and content pattern to support YPP eligibility.
- Post consistently enough to build audience and history.
- Screen for sensitive content (especially war footage) before posting.
- Choose sources deliberately (avoid sources with known enforcement posture).
- Log everything — sourcing, overlay, sensitive-content, platform response.
- Possibly evolve the format if eligibility becomes the binding constraint (more original framing, more value-add, possibly voiceover/commentary later).

### Ranking rationale

YPP is ranked first as the primary monetization target because it's the most scalable and most direct route if the channel qualifies — but it's ranked first with the explicit caveat that eligibility is the gating question, not a given. The ranking reflects the upside, not certainty.

## Route 2 — Off-Platform Ownership (Newsletter / Site / Other Channel)

### What it is

Building an owned audience layer — a newsletter, a site, or another channel — that the venture controls outright. This is not platform monetization; it's an asset the venture owns, with its own monetization potential (direct monetization, sponsorships, products, audience data).

### Fit with this channel

**High fit. Strategic, not immediate.**

- **Surface fit:** Independent of platform. The channel can collect audience permission wherever it has traction.
- **Format fit:** Independent of format. The owned layer can carry clips, analysis, context, and more — whatever the channel evolves toward.
- **Genre fit:** Independent of genre. The owned layer can be positioned around the channel's brand and angle.
- **Zero-budget fit:** Very high. Starting a newsletter or simple site is inexpensive. Collecting audience permission is free. This is a route that fits the zero-budget constraint well.

### Speed to first revenue

**Slow (strategic, not lucrative — yet).**

An owned audience layer pays off when there's an audience to own. On a test batch, there's no audience to collect. Building it early is strategic value, not revenue. First revenue from the owned layer is later, after the audience exists.

### Sustainability and scale

**High, as a hedge and a long-term lever.**

- The owned layer survives platform changes. If YouTube changes eligibility rules, if TikTok changes enforcement, if a platform removes the channel — the owned layer remains.
- The owned layer broadens the monetization surface. It can support direct monetization, sponsorships, products, and more.
- The owned layer is an asset the venture owns outright, not something it rents on a platform.

### Downside and risk

- **No immediate revenue.** This is a strategic play, not a quick monetization route. The CFO's recommendation is to start early because of strategic value, not because it pays now.
- **Requires audience to be valuable.** An empty owned layer has no monetization value. It grows with the channel.
- **Requires ongoing effort.** An owned layer needs content, maintenance, and audience engagement. It's not set-and-forget.

### What it requires from the operator

- Pick a layer (newsletter, site, or other channel).
- Start simple — don't overbuild.
- Collect audience permission as the channel grows (email, follows, whatever the layer uses).
- Maintain the layer as the channel grows.

### Ranking rationale

Ranked second because it's the highest-strategic-value route that fits the zero-budget constraint, but ranked second because it's not immediate revenue. It's a hedge and a long-term lever, not a quick monetization path. The CFO recommends starting it early for strategic reasons, not because it pays now.

## Route 3 — TikTok Creator Programs

### What it is

TikTok's creator monetization programs (Creator rewards, Creator Marketplace, and other program-specific features). Each has eligibility requirements — account standing, originality/reused-content expectations, engagement thresholds, geographic and age requirements.

### Fit with this channel

**Medium fit, with gating.**

- **Surface fit:** The channel is live on TikTok, which is the primary test surface and the fastest-reach surface. Creator programs are a direct monetization path if eligible.
- **Format fit:** Reused-content and compilation patterns can affect eligibility. The channel's third-party-footage model is in the zone these expectations target. Eligibility is not automatic.
- **Genre fit:** War content is sensitive on TikTok; tech/trending less so but not zero. Sensitivity affects eligibility and reach, which affects monetization.

### Speed to first revenue

**Medium (later eligibility problem, not immediate).**

TikTok monetization eligibility is a separate gate from reach. A clip can reach an audience and still be ineligible. The eligibility is shaped by account history and content pattern over time. Realistic timing is later, not immediate.

### Sustainability and scale

**Medium.**

TikTok monetization can be a meaningful revenue path if eligible, but it's generally less scalable and less mature than YouTube's monetization infrastructure. It's a path, not the primary long-game anchor.

### Downside and risk

- **Eligibility gating.** Reused-content and compilation expectations can affect eligibility.
- **Sensitivity enforcement.** War content can be removed, age-gated, or throttled on TikTok.
- **Platform dependency.** TikTok controls the monetization. Changes to rules or enforcement can affect revenue.
- **Reach volatility.** TikTok reach can be volatile; monetization tied to reach is volatile.

### What it requires from the operator

- Build account history and content pattern toward eligibility.
- Screen for sensitive content.
- Source deliberately.
- Observe eligibility signals.

### Ranking rationale

Ranked third as a monetization path on the primary test surface, but secondary to YPP as the long-game anchor. It's a path to build toward, not the primary monetization strategy.

## Route 4 — Instagram Monetization Features

### What it is

Instagram's monetization features (bonuses, gifts, subscriptions, branded content, and other program-specific features). Each has eligibility criteria — original-content expectations, account standing, program-specific requirements.

### Fit with this channel

**Low-to-medium fit, secondary surface.**

- **Surface fit:** The channel is live on Instagram. Reels is a distribution surface.
- **Format fit:** Repurposed/unoriginal content can face reduced distribution and feature eligibility. The channel's model is in the zone of concern, but the risk is often more about reach/feature eligibility than immediate takedown.
- **Genre fit:** Less sensitive than war content on Instagram generally, but not zero.
- **Strategic fit:** Per the brief, Instagram is a distribution surface with a different audience, not the primary monetization anchor.

### Speed to first revenue

**Slow (secondary surface, program-specific).**

Monetization on Instagram is program-specific and gated. Treat as a secondary, later path.

### Sustainability and scale

**Low-to-medium.**

Instagram monetization features can contribute, but they're not the primary revenue engine for this channel. The channel's Instagram strategy is distribution and account health first; monetization is secondary.

### Downside and risk

- **Program-specific eligibility.** Not automatic.
- **Reach/feature eligibility.** Repurposed content can face reduced distribution and reduced feature eligibility.
- **Secondary priority.** Don't over-index on Instagram monetization.

### What it requires from the operator

- Maintain distribution health and account standing.
- Observe eligibility signals.
- Don't over-index.

### Ranking rationale

Ranked fourth as a secondary surface per the brief. Not the primary focus.

## Route 5 — Sponsorship / Affiliate

### What it is

Direct sponsorship, brand deals, and affiliate revenue. This is the broadest long-term monetization surface for many channels, but it requires an audience to exist first and a fit between the audience and the sponsor.

### Fit with this channel

**Medium-to-high fit long-term, given genre expansion.**

- **Genre fit:** The genre expansion to tech + viral news broadens the sponsor/affiliate surface vs. war-only. Tech brands, product launches, software, consumer tech, and news-adjacent sponsors are more available than war/news sponsors.
- **Audience fit:** Unknown at this stage. The channel has zero audience. Sponsorship value depends on the audience the channel builds and its fit with sponsor categories.
- **Format fit:** A consistent, describable format helps sponsor-readiness. A channel that's chaotic or primarily reposting may be harder to sponsor.

### Speed to first revenue

**Slow (requires audience first).**

Sponsors want an audience. Sponsorship value comes from reach, engagement, and audience fit — not from a handful of test clips. First sponsorship revenue is later, after the channel has an audience and a fit.

### Sustainability and scale

**High, long-term.**

If the channel builds an audience with a fit for sponsor categories, sponsorship can be a significant and scalable revenue path. It's the route with the broadest long-term surface given the genre expansion.

### Downside and risk

- **Audience-first.** No audience, no sponsors. This is a later route.
- **Sponsor category limitations.** War/news content can limit some sponsor categories regardless of the broader genre mix.
- **Brand alignment risk.** Sponsorship introduces brand-alignment considerations. A channel that takes sponsorship must consider fit — including for war/news content, where some sponsors may not want association.
- **Platform dependency for reach.** Sponsorship value often depends on platform reach, which is platform-controlled.

### What it requires from the operator

- Build an audience first.
- Maintain sponsor-readiness (clean branding, consistent format, trackable audience data, a describable channel).
- Don't pitch sponsorship on a test batch.
- Consider brand alignment as sponsorship becomes possible.

### Ranking rationale

Ranked fifth as a broad long-term surface that's real but requires audience first. The genre expansion makes it more viable than it would be for war-only, but it's still later.

## Route 6 — Productized Offerings / Own Product

### What it is

A product layered on top of the channel — a subscription, a curated feed, a premium tier, a paid newsletter, a tool, or other productized offering.

### Fit with this channel

**Low-to-medium fit at this stage. Ambitionally possible, operationally distant.**

- **Audience fit:** Requires an audience first. A product needs people willing to pay; that requires trust, value, and an audience.
- **Format fit:** Possible if the channel builds a position around analysis, context, curation, or insight — something a product could deliver. A pure reposting channel is harder to productize.
- **Operational fit:** A product business layered on an audience business requires product execution capacity. At zero budget and on a test batch, this is not near-term.

### Speed to first revenue

**Slow (requires audience + product thesis + execution).**

A product is not a near-term monetization path for this venture at its current stage.

### Sustainability and scale

**High, if it works.** A successful product can be a major revenue lever and an owned asset. But "if it works" is the operative phrase.

### Downside and risk

- **Requires audience and product execution.** Not near-term.
- **Opportunity cost.** Time spent on a product thesis at this stage is time not spent on the clips, the workflow, the audience, and the eligibility trajectory.
- **Distraction risk.** A premature product focus can distract from the audience-building that a product needs.

### What it requires from the operator

- Build an audience first.
- Develop a product thesis only when there's a clear reason (an audience that wants something, a gap the product fills).
- Keep the channel structured so a product layer is possible later (owned audience, clean branding, trust).

### Ranking rationale

Ranked sixth as a lower-priority, later route. Ambitionally possible, but not the near-term path. The CFO's recommendation is to keep the channel structured for it, not to build a product strategy on a test batch.

## Ranking Rationale (Summary)

The ranking is not a promise that any route will pay — it's a ranking of where to focus attention and build toward, given the venture's constraints.

1. **YPP is ranked first** because it's the most scalable and most direct route if the channel qualifies — but with the explicit caveat that eligibility is the gating question, not a given.
2. **Off-platform ownership is ranked second** because it's the highest-strategic-value route that fits zero budget, and it should start early — but it's not immediate revenue.
3. **TikTok Creator Programs is ranked third** as a monetization path on the primary test surface, but secondary to YPP as the long-game anchor.
4. **Instagram is ranked fourth** as a secondary surface per the brief.
5. **Sponsorship is ranked fifth** as a broad long-term surface made more viable by the genre expansion, but requiring audience first.
6. **Product is ranked sixth** as a later, operationally distant route the channel should keep open but not pursue on a test batch.
