## How to Use This File

For each platform, the file covers:
- The monetization program(s) that matter
- The eligibility requirements (structural — what the platform says)
- How the channel's format (third-party footage, text overlays) interacts with those requirements
- How the channel's genre (war/news + tech + viral) interacts with those requirements
- How the zero-budget constraint interacts with those requirements

The structural requirements are stated as structure, not as fixed numbers — thresholds and mechanics change over time and by program version. The channel-specific assessment is where the analysis gets specific, and where the confidence levels matter.

## Confidence Convention for This File

- **High confidence:** Well-established structural rule (e.g., YPP has eligibility thresholds; reused-content rules exist; sensitive-content rules exist).
- **Medium confidence:** Established framework with fact-specific application or evolving detail (e.g., how a specific content pattern will be treated, exact current thresholds, how account history interacts with per-clip outcomes).
- **Low confidence:** Uncertain, evolving, or dependent on facts not yet known (e.g., the specific outcome for this channel's exact content mix at this stage).

## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP)

### Monetization program(s) that matter

- **YouTube Partner Program** — the primary monetization program. Ads on long-form and Shorts; Shorts-specific monetization mechanics; eligibility thresholds; reused-content and repetitive-content policies; advertiser-friendly content considerations.

### Eligibility requirements (structural)

YPP eligibility is gated. The structural elements include:
- **Eligibility thresholds** — subscribers, watch time / Shorts views. The exact numbers and mechanics change over time and by program version.
- **Originality / reused-content standing** — channels that largely post others' content without sufficient commentary, educational value, or narrative can be denied monetization or removed from YPP, even if the content is not necessarily copyright-infringing.
- **Repetitive content** — a large volume of highly similar content can limit monetization.
- **Account standing and policy compliance** — the channel must be in good standing.
- **Advertiser-friendly content considerations** — content that is not advertiser-friendly may be limited in monetization even if eligible.

### How the channel's format interacts

**This is the central eligibility question for this channel.**

The channel's format — third-party footage (broadcast, wire, other creators) with text overlays, no voiceover, no commentary, no on-camera — is the format YPP's reused-content expectations most directly scrutinize. A channel that primarily posts cut clips with thin overlays may fall into the zone YPP targets.

The text overlays add some transformation, but the degree of transformation matters. A headline and timestamp over a wholesale clip is thinner than a headline, context, attribution, and framing that re-purposes the clip as reporting on the event. The eligibility outcome is clip-pattern-level and channel-pattern-level, not clip-by-clip in isolation — the overall character of the channel's output matters.

**The honest assessment:** On the current format, YPP eligibility is uncertain, not assured. The channel may be able to build toward eligibility by strengthening the transformation posture (more value-adding overlays, more original framing, possibly voiceover/commentary later), by developing some original content alongside the clips, or by establishing a pattern that reads as more original than reposting. Or the channel may find that the current format is not eligible on YPP, in which case the format or content strategy needs to evolve.

### How the channel's genre interacts

- **War/news content:** Can be advertiser-sensitive. Graphic content can be age-restricted or demonetized. This affects monetization eligibility and revenue potential independently of the originality question.
- **Tech content:** Generally more advertiser-friendly. Tech product announcements, executive statements, product analysis — these are often cleaner from an advertiser-friendliness perspective than graphic war footage.
- **Viral/trending news:** Variable. Depends on the content. Can be advertiser-friendly or can carry sensitivity issues.

The genre mix matters. A channel that is mostly war footage with graphic content faces more advertiser-friendliness headwinds than a channel with a mix that includes tech and less-sensitive viral news.

### How the zero-budget constraint interacts

Zero budget means the channel can't license sources to clear rights. It also means the channel can't buy its way out of eligibility problems. The only tools are source selection, transformation posture, content pattern, and time. The channel cannot pay for a faster path to eligibility.

### Eligibility risk for this channel

**Medium-to-high risk, given the current format.**

The format is in the zone YPP scrutinizes. The channel starts at zero audience and zero history. The genre mix includes war content, which adds advertiser-friendliness headwinds. The channel has not yet established a content pattern that demonstrates originality.

**Mitigating factors:**
- YouTube is also the surface where the channel can most naturally build a longer-form or more original presence over time.
- The channel can evolve the format (more value-adding overlays, original framing, possibly voiceover/commentary later) to strengthen the eligibility trajectory.
- Tech and less-sensitive viral content can balance the war content for advertiser-friendliness.
- Eligibility is earned over time, and the channel is just starting — the assessment is a snapshot, not a final verdict.

**Confidence:** Medium-to-high on the structural risk (the format is in the zone of concern); low-to-medium on the specific outcome for this channel at this stage, because that depends on the content pattern the channel builds over time and on rules that evolve.

## 2. TikTok Creator Programs

### Monetization program(s) that matter

- **TikTok Creator rewards / Creator Marketplace** and other program-specific features. Eligibility requirements include account standing, originality/reused-content expectations, engagement thresholds, and geographic/age requirements.

### Eligibility requirements (structural)

- **Account standing and policy compliance.**
- **Originality / reused-content expectations** — uploading content that is not original to the account, especially in patterns that suggest mass reposting or compilation of others' content, can affect eligibility.
- **Engagement and other program-specific thresholds.**
- **Geographic and age requirements.**

### How the channel's format interacts

The channel's third-party-footage model is in the zone TikTok's reused-content enforcement targets. If the channel's output reads as "reposting others' clips with minimal addition," it is in the zone. The more the channel can demonstrate originality in selection, editing, overlays, and context, the more it moves away from that zone — but the model does not leave the zone entirely on the current format.

The text overlays add some originality, but the same caveat applies as on YouTube: a headline and timestamp over a wholesale clip is thinner than substantial added context and framing.

### How the channel's genre interacts

- **War content:** Aggressive and partly automated enforcement on TikTok. War footage can be removed, age-gated, or throttled. This affects eligibility and reach.
- **Tech/trending:** Less sensitive than war content, but not zero. Reused-content patterns still apply.

### How the zero-budget constraint interacts

Same as YouTube — the channel can't license or pay its way out. Only source selection, transformation posture, content pattern, and time.

### Eligibility risk for this channel

**Medium-to-high risk, given the current format and the war content.**

TikTok's reused-content expectations and sensitivity enforcement both apply. The channel is on the primary test surface, which means the earliest signals will come here — including eligibility signals.

**Confidence:** Medium-to-high on the structural risk; low-to-medium on the specific outcome at this stage.

## 4. Cross-Platform Eligibility Themes

### Theme 1 — "Live" is not "monetizable"

A clip can be live on all three platforms and still be throttled, age-gated, or excluded from monetization eligibility on each. The eligibility gate is separate from the publishing gate. This is the single most important eligibility theme for this channel: the workflow should not assume that publishing equals monetization eligibility.

### Theme 2 — Reused-content/originality is the central eligibility question

Across platforms, the channel's third-party-footage model is the format most exposed to the originality/reused-content expectations that gate monetization. The text overlays add some transformation, but the degree of transformation matters, and the channel's overall content pattern matters. This is the eligibility question the channel must answer — either by building toward eligibility within the current format, or by evolving the format.

### Theme 3 — Sensitivity is a separate, fast failure mode

War content's graphic/sensitive nature can remove, age-gate, or throttle clips on all three platforms, independently of copyright or originality. This affects reach, eligibility, and revenue. It's the fastest failure mode for war content, and it's a first-order risk for the channel's war clips.

### Theme 4 — Eligibility is earned over time, not granted on day one

All three platforms' monetization eligibility is earned over time, subject to content-pattern review, account history, and evolving rules. The channel starts at zero audience and zero history. The realistic framing is a trajectory, not an event.

### Theme 5 — Rules change

Platform eligibility rules, thresholds, and enforcement evolve. A channel that is fine under today's rules can become non-eligible under tomorrow's. This is a reason to build with margin and to revisit eligibility as the channel scales or the format changes.

## 5. Eligibility Roadmap (Sequence, Not Promise)

This is the sequence the channel should build toward. It is not a promise that any of these will clear on this timeline — it's the path to give the best chance.

### Phase 1 — Test Batch (Days 1–14)

**Eligibility goal:** Learn. No monetization revenue expected. Observe eligibility signals.

- Produce the test batch with the best overlay craft the operator can do.
- Source deliberately (Tier 1 where available, selective Tier 2, no Tier 4).
- Screen for sensitive content before posting.
- Observe platform eligibility signals — what gets flagged, what gets throttled, what stays clean, what gets age-gated.

### Phase 2 — Early Channel (Days 15–30)

**Eligibility goal:** Build toward eligibility. First revenue is not expected; eligibility trajectory is.

- Carry forward the format that showed signal.
- Improve overlay design based on test data.
- Adjust source mix based on what behaved as expected.
- Scale to a cadence the labor model supports.
- Begin treating YouTube as the eligibility trajectory surface.
- Observe eligibility signals on each platform.

### Phase 3 — Build Toward Monetization (Days 31–90)

**Eligibility goal:** Reach eligibility gates where possible; grow the owned layer; develop sponsor-readiness.

- Continue the working format and sustainable cadence.
- Build toward YPP eligibility (and TikTok/Instagram eligibility where in range).
- Test eligibility gates as they come into view.
- If eligibility is blocked on the current format, evaluate format evolution (more original framing, more value-add, possibly voiceover/commentary later).

## 6. The Eligibility Decision Point

The eligibility analysis surfaces a decision the channel must make, possibly more than once:

**If the current format (third-party footage, text overlays, no voiceover, no commentary) cannot establish sufficient originality/reused-content standing for the platform monetization programs the channel wants, what does the channel do?**

The options include:
- Evolve the overlay design to add more value and transformation (stronger overlays, more context, more original framing).
- Add some original content alongside the clips (analysis, context, reporting) to strengthen the channel's originality pattern.
- Add voiceover or commentary on some or all clips (if the operator is willing) — this strengthens the transformation posture and the originality pattern.
- Accept that the channel's monetization may be limited on platform programs and lean more on off-platform ownership and sponsorship (which have different eligibility dynamics).
- Pivot the genre mix toward more eligible content (more tech, less graphic war content) if that's what the eligibility landscape requires.

The decision is not a one-time decision. It's a decision the channel revisits as the eligibility landscape becomes clearer and as the channel builds its content pattern.

## 7. What This Analysis Does Not Resolve

- The specific eligibility outcome for this channel on any platform. That depends on the content pattern the channel builds over time and on rules that evolve.
- The exact current thresholds and mechanics. These change over time and by program version. The structural landscape is stable; the precise numbers are not.
- Whether the channel will actually monetize. Eligibility is necessary but not sufficient — the channel also needs an audience, and monetization depends on more than eligibility.
