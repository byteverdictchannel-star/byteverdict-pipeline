## How to Use This Dossier

This dossier documents every material assumption in the sensitivity analysis, with:
- The driver
- The impact assessment
- The source or basis for the assessment
- The confidence level
- What would change the assessment

The point is to make the sensitivity transparent and challengeable. The CFO's sensitivity is only as good as the assumptions behind it — and the most important drivers are the ones with the highest impact and the lowest confidence.

## Driver Inventory

### Driver 1 — Eligibility (platform monetization)

**Impact: Very high.** If the channel can't clear platform monetization eligibility, the platform revenue side is much lower or zero.

**Source / basis:** Structural eligibility landscape (platform documentation) + inference from the channel's format and content pattern. The eligibility analysis (`02-analysis/02-platform-eligibility.md`) documents the structural landscape; the channel-specific application is inference.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** High. **Confidence on the specific outcome for this channel:** Low-to-medium — depends on the content pattern the channel builds and on rules that evolve.

**What would change this:** Evolving platform rules; the channel's content pattern over time; a decision to evolve the format (stronger overlays, original framing, voiceover/commentary, original content alongside clips).

### Driver 2 — Audience growth

**Impact: Very high.** Audience is the precondition for monetization across all routes. No audience, no platform revenue (no views), no off-platform value, no sponsorship, no product.

**Source / basis:** Structural reality (monetization requires an audience) + the channel's unknown audience trajectory. The trend synthesis (`02-analysis/03-trend-synthesis.md`) documents the structural reality; the channel-specific trajectory is unknown.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** High. **Confidence on the specific audience growth for this channel:** Low — the biggest unknown at this stage.

**What would change this:** Finding a format/genre mix that gets traction; iterating on overlays and sourcing; platform dynamics; the operator's consistency and cadence.

### Driver 3 — Per-clip time (operational cost)

**Impact: High.** The per-clip time drives the cost side of the economics.

**Source / basis:** Operational reality (the channel's per-clip time is the cost driver) + the unknown per-clip time. The unit economics assumptions dossier (`03-dossiers/04-unit-economics-assumptions.md`) documents the per-clip time assumption as the most important operational assumption to measure.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** High. **Confidence on the specific per-clip time:** Low currently (to be measured in the test batch).

**What would change this:** Better sourcing workflow; overlay templates; batch editing; subagent help where it fits; sustainable cadence; tools and workflow improvements.

### Driver 4 — Genre mix over time

**Impact: Medium-to-high.** The genre mix affects advertiser-friendliness, sensitivity profile, sponsor fit, and platform treatment.

**Source / basis:** Structural reality (different genres have different monetization surfaces, sponsor categories, sensitivity profiles, and platform treatments) + the trend synthesis (`02-analysis/03-trend-synthesis.md`). The channel-specific mix is unknown.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** Medium-to-high. **Confidence on the specific genre mix the channel will land on:** Low-to-medium — depends on what the operator chooses and what the data shows.

**What would change this:** The operator leaning toward more tech/viral and less graphic war content; data showing which genres perform and which get flagged.

### Driver 5 — Platform volatility / rule changes

**Impact: Medium-to-high.** Platforms change rules, thresholds, eligibility, and enforcement. A channel that is fine under today's rules can become non-eligible or non-monetizable under tomorrow's.

**Source / basis:** Structural reality (platforms change) + the channel's lack of control over platform decisions. The platform rule excerpts dossier (`03-dossiers/02-platform-rule-excerpts.md`) documents the structural landscape and the "rules change" theme.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** Medium-to-high. **Confidence on specific future changes:** Low — unknowable.

**What would change this:** Nothing the channel can control. The channel can build with margin, diversify across routes and platforms, and keep the owned layer as a hedge.

### Driver 6 — Sponsor fit

**Impact: Medium (long-term).** If the audience fits sponsor categories well, sponsorship adds a revenue lever. If not, it doesn't.

**Source / basis:** Structural reality (sponsorship requires audience and fit) + the trend synthesis (`02-analysis/03-trend-synthesis.md`) + the genre expansion (tech + viral broadens sponsor surface). The channel-specific sponsor fit is unknown.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** Medium (long-term). **Confidence on the specific sponsor fit:** Low-to-medium — depends on the audience and genre mix.

**What would change this:** The audience and genre mix the channel lands on; the operator's positioning choices.

### Driver 7 — Opportunity cost of the operator's time

**Impact: Personal/strategic, not market.** The operator's time has an opportunity cost. If the time invested in this channel is not the best use of that time relative to alternatives, the channel's economics are negative even if it eventually monetizes.

**Source / basis:** Strategic reality (time is scarce capital with opportunity cost) + the operator's personal/strategic valuation. The unit economics assumptions dossier (`03-dossiers/04-unit-economics-assumptions.md`) documents this as the most important strategic assumption.

**Confidence on the driver's importance:** High (as a strategic question). **Confidence on the specific valuation:** Low — it's a personal/strategic decision, not a market price.

**What would change this:** The operator's decision about whether this channel is the best use of their time.

## Scenario Assumptions

### Base case assumptions (illustrative)

- The channel builds a modest audience over time on at least one surface.
- The channel reaches some level of platform monetization eligibility on at least one platform, possibly after format or content-pattern evolution.
- Per-clip time is moderate and the workflow becomes sustainable.
- The genre mix includes enough tech/less-sensitive content to keep sensitivity and advertiser-friendliness manageable.
- The owned layer grows with the audience.
- Sponsorship becomes possible after the audience reaches a certain size and fit.

**Confidence on this scenario as a forecast:** Low. It's an illustrative base case, not a prediction.

### Downside case assumptions (illustrative)

- The channel struggles to build an audience (the format or genre mix doesn't get traction).
- The channel's format can't clear platform monetization eligibility, or eligibility is delayed significantly.
- Per-clip time is high and the workflow is not sustainable at scale.
- The channel gets flagged or age-gated on war clips repeatedly, limiting reach and eligibility.
- Platform rules change in ways that hurt the channel's eligibility or reach.

**Confidence on this scenario as a forecast:** Low. It's an illustrative downside, not a prediction. But it's a real possibility the CFO forces the CEO and operator to consider.

### Upside case assumptions (illustrative)

- The channel finds a format and genre mix that gets real traction and builds an audience.
- The channel reaches platform monetization eligibility and generates revenue.
- Per-clip time is manageable and the workflow scales.
- The owned layer grows and provides a direct monetization path.
- Sponsorship becomes viable because the audience fits sponsor categories.
- The channel avoids major flags, claims, or enforcement events.

**Confidence on this scenario as a forecast:** Low. It's an illustrative upside, not a prediction. But it's the case the channel is building toward.

## Break-Point Assumptions

### Break point 1 — No audience

**Assumption:** If the channel can't build an audience, the revenue side is much lower across all routes.

**Source / basis:** Structural reality (audience is the precondition for monetization). The trend synthesis documents this.

**Confidence on the break point's importance:** High. **Confidence on whether this channel will hit it:** Low.

### Break point 2 — Eligibility blocked

**Assumption:** If the channel's format can't clear platform monetization eligibility, the platform revenue side is much lower.

**Source / basis:** Structural eligibility landscape (platform documentation) + inference from the channel's format. The eligibility analysis documents this.

**Confidence on the break point's importance:** High. **Confidence on whether this channel will hit it:** Low-to-medium.

### Break point 3 — Per-clip time too high

**Assumption:** If producing a clip takes too much time, the channel needs more revenue or a more efficient workflow to justify the time.

**Source / basis:** Operational reality (per-clip time is the cost driver). The unit economics assumptions dossier documents per-clip time as the most important operational assumption to measure.

**Confidence on the break point's importance:** High. **Confidence on the specific per-clip time:** Low (to be measured).

### Break point 4 — Platform volatility breaks the revenue

**Assumption:** If platforms change rules or enforcement in ways that hurt the channel, the revenue side can change unexpectedly.

**Source / basis:** Structural reality (platforms change). The platform rule excerpts dossier documents the "rules change" theme.

**Confidence on the break point's importance:** Medium-to-high. **Confidence on specific future changes:** Low.

### Break point 5 — The time is the wrong investment

**Assumption:** If the operator's time is not the best use of their time relative to alternatives, the channel's economics are negative even if it eventually monetizes.

**Source / basis:** Strategic reality (time is scarce capital with opportunity cost). The unit economics assumptions dossier documents this as the most important strategic assumption.

**Confidence on the break point's importance:** High (as a strategic question). **Confidence on whether this channel is the wrong investment:** Low — it's the operator's decision.
