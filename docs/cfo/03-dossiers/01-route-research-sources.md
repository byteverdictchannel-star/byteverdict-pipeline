## How to Use This Dossier

This dossier is the provenance layer for the route analysis. It documents:
- Where the financial and eligibility information came from
- What is established fact vs. what is directional synthesis
- What would need to be verified or updated
- What is Uber strawman or illustrative vs. grounded

The point is not to produce a bibliography for its own sake. The point is to make the route analysis traceable, challengeable, and updatable — so the CEO and operator can see where the numbers and conclusions come from, and so the CFO can update them as new information arrives.

## Source Categories

### A. Platform monetization documentation (primary structural source)

The structural eligibility requirements and monetization mechanics for YouTube Partner Program, TikTok Creator programs, and Instagram monetization features come from the platforms' own documentation — terms of service, partner program policies, creator program documentation, community guidelines, and monetization feature documentation.

**Confidence on the structural information:** High — this is what the platforms say, and it is the most direct source for the eligibility landscape.

**Limits:** Platform documentation changes over time; exact thresholds and mechanics vary by program version and geography; platforms do not publish a bright line for "how much transformation is enough"; enforcement behavior is partly automated and partly opaque.

**What was used:** The structural eligibility landscape — thresholds exist, reused-content/originality rules exist, sensitivity rules exist, eligibility is earned over time, monetization is gated — is grounded in the platforms' documented frameworks. The channel-specific application is inference from that structure, not from the platforms' documentation of this channel.

### B. Creator economy / clips channel market observations (directional source)

Observations about what's working for clips channels, what formats tend to perform, what monetization paths tend to be viable, and what eligibility patterns tend to apply come from the broader creator economy and clips-channel market — observable patterns, not a systematic survey of every channel.

**Confidence on directional observations:** Medium — the general patterns are reasonably established (platform monetization is gated; reused-content is the central eligibility question; sensitive content is a fast failure mode for war content; sponsorship requires an audience; owned layer is a hedge), but the specific implications for this channel are inference and vary by channel, genre, and platform version.

**Limits:** Market observations are directional, not deterministic. What works for one channel may not work for another. Platform behavior varies. Genre behavior varies. Geography varies.

**What was used:** The trend synthesis (`03-trend-synthesis.md`) is grounded in these directional observations. The route analysis draws on them for fit assessments and ranking rationale.

### C. Financial modeling frameworks (methodological source)

The financial modeling approach — unit economics, per-clip cost/revenue decomposition, scenario analysis, sensitivity, opportunity cost of time — draws on standard financial modeling frameworks (unit economics: CAC/LTV/payback/contribution margin; scenario planning: base/downside/upside; sensitivity: top drivers and impact ranges).

**Confidence on the methodology:** High — these are established financial modeling frameworks.

**Limits:** The frameworks are applied to a venture with zero data (no audience, no revenue, no measured per-clip time). The models are illustrative under stated assumptions, not forecasts. The methodology is sound; the inputs are mostly unknowns.

**What was used:** The unit economics file (`02-analysis/04-unit-economics.md`) and sensitivity file (`02-analysis/05-sensitivity.md`) are grounded in these frameworks. The methodological soundness is high; the input uncertainty is the honest limitation.

### D. Legal / platform risk landscape (companion source)

The legal and platform risk landscape comes from the Legal & Risk Landscape Report (`~/clips-channel/legal-report/00-index.md`), which is the dedicated analysis of copyright, platform policy, and sensitive-content risk. The CFO references that report as a key input; this dossier does not re-derive the legal analysis.

**Confidence on the legal input:** As documented in the legal report — high on established principles (attribution is not a defense; clipping is copying; fair use is fact-specific; platform policies are independent from copyright; sensitive content is a first-order risk for war footage), medium-to-low on the channel-specific application.

**What was used:** The CFO's route analysis, eligibility analysis, and trend synthesis reference the legal report for copyright, platform policy, and sensitive-content risk that affects monetization eligibility and sustainability. The CFO does not resolve legal questions; it flags them and incorporates the legal report's confidence levels.

### E. Zero-budget constraint (venture-specific source)

The zero-budget constraint is a venture-specific fact — the channel has no licensing money, no tooling spend, no outsourcing spend to start. This is the defining constraint of the venture and the source of the "real cost is time" framing.

**Confidence on the constraint:** High — it's a stated venture fact.

**What was used:** The entire monetization analysis is conditioned on zero budget. The route assessment, eligibility assessment, unit economics, and sensitivity all reflect the zero-budget constraint.

### F. Genre and format facts (venture-specific source)

The genre (war/news + tech + viral, highly clickable) and format (cut + text overlay, no voiceover, no on-camera for now) are venture-specific facts from the planning brief. They are the inputs that shape the channel-specific assessments.

**Confidence on the facts:** High — they're stated venture facts.

**What was used:** The route fit assessments, the eligibility application, the trend synthesis implications, and the unit economics all depend on these facts.

## What Is Established vs. Directional vs. Illustrative

### Established (high confidence)

- Platform monetization is gated and earned over time.
- Reused-content/originality rules exist across platforms and are the central eligibility question for clips channels.
- Sensitive-content enforcement is a first-order risk for war footage, independent of copyright.
- Attribution is not a legal or platform defense.
- Licensing requires budget; zero budget means no licensing.
- Sponsorship requires an audience; owned layer is a strategic hedge; platform volatility is a real risk.
- Financial modeling frameworks (unit economics, scenarios, sensitivity) are established methodology.

### Directional (medium confidence)

- The degree of transformation that tends to help with eligibility and reach (more value-adding overlay, commentary, context, originality).
- The general sponsor fit dynamics by genre (tech + viral broader than war-only).
- The general sensitivity profile by genre (war high, tech lower, viral variable).
- The general observation that platforms apply originality/reused-content expectations at the account/content-pattern level, not just clip-by-clip.
- That eligibility is earned over time and subject to content-pattern review.

### Illustrative (low confidence as forecast)

- The specific per-clip time for this channel (to be measured).
- The specific eligibility outcome for this channel (depends on content pattern and evolving rules).
- The specific audience this channel will build (the biggest unknown).
- The specific revenue per clip or per channel (depends on eligibility, audience, and platform mechanics).
- The specific sponsor fit for this channel (depends on audience and genre mix).
- The specific break-even point for this channel (depends on all of the above).

## What Needs Verification or Updating

The following should be verified or updated as the venture progresses:

1. **Current platform eligibility thresholds and mechanics.** These change over time and by program version. The structural landscape is stable; the precise numbers should be verified against current documentation when the channel reaches eligibility-application stage.
2. **Current platform enforcement behavior for this content mix.** Sensitivity enforcement and reused-content enforcement are partly automated and partly opaque. As the channel posts and observes platform response, the assessment should be updated.
3. **Per-clip time.** To be measured in the test batch. This is the key operational assumption to replace with actual data.
4. **Audience response.** As the channel posts and observes audience response, the trend synthesis and unit economics should be updated.
5. **Sponsor category fit.** As the channel builds an audience and develops a genre mix, the sponsor fit assessment should be updated.
6. **Legal landscape.** As the legal report notes, the landscape evolves. The CFO should revisit the legal input when the format changes, the source mix changes, the monetization path changes, or the audience geography becomes defined.

## Data Provenance Convention

- **Platform documentation** is the source for structural eligibility information. Where a specific platform rule is cited, it should be traceable to the platform's documentation (see `03-dossiers/02-platform-rule-excerpts.md`).
- **Market observations** are directional. Where a market observation is used as an input, it is labeled as directional synthesis, not as established fact about this channel.
- **Venture-specific facts** (zero budget, genre, format, accounts live) are from the planning brief. They are the inputs, with high confidence.
- **Financial modeling frameworks** are methodological. They are established; the application to this venture is illustrative under stated assumptions.
- **Legal landscape** is from the legal report. The CFO references it; it does not re-derive it.
