## How to Use This Dossier

This dossier is the structural reference for the eligibility analysis. For each platform, it captures:
- The monetization program(s) that matter
- The eligibility requirements that bear on this channel
- The reused-content/originality rules that bear on this channel
- The sensitive-content rules that bear on this channel
- The monetization-eligibility rules that bear on this channel
- The structural point the rule makes for this channel

Where a specific rule or threshold is cited, it should be traceable to the platform's documentation. Where the rule is structural (e.g., "eligibility is gated"), the citation is to the platform's framework, not necessarily to a fixed number.

## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP)

### 1.1 Monetization program

YouTube Partner Program. Ads on long-form and Shorts; Shorts-specific monetization mechanics; eligibility thresholds; reused-content and repetitive-content policies; advertiser-friendly content considerations.

### 1.2 Structural eligibility requirements

- **Eligibility thresholds.** YPP has eligibility thresholds (subscribers, watch time / Shorts views). The exact numbers and mechanics change over time and by program version.
- **Originality / reused-content standing.** Channels that largely post others' content without sufficient commentary, educational value, or narrative can be denied monetization or removed from YPP, even if the content is not necessarily copyright-infringing.
- **Repetitive content.** A large volume of highly similar content can limit monetization.
- **Account standing and policy compliance.** The channel must be in good standing.
- **Advertiser-friendly content considerations.** Content that is not advertiser-friendly may be limited in monetization even if eligible.

**Structural point for this channel:** YPP eligibility is gated, and the channel's format (third-party footage, text overlays) is in the zone the reused-content/originality expectations scrutinize. Eligibility is earned over time and subject to content-pattern review. A clip being live is not the same as being monetizable.

### 1.3 Reused-content / originality rules

YPP's reused-content and repetitive-content policies target channels that largely post others' content without sufficient addition. The structural point is that originality and transformation matter for eligibility — not just that the content is not copyright-infringing.

**Structural point for this channel:** The channel's text-overlay-only, third-party-footage format is the format most exposed to these expectations. The degree of transformation (stronger overlays, more context, more original framing, possibly voiceover/commentary or original content later) is the lever that affects this.

### 1.4 Sensitive-content / advertiser-friendly rules

YouTube's policies address violent or graphic content. War footage can be age-restricted, removed (if it violates policies on gratuitous or shocking violence), or demonetized/limited in advertiser-friendliness. Age-restriction can effectively remove a Short from the main Shorts distribution path and from monetization eligibility, even if it remains technically live.

**Structural point for this channel:** War content on YouTube carries a sensitivity risk that is separate from the originality question. A war clip can have a strong overlay and still be age-restricted or demonetized for graphic content. This affects both reach and monetization eligibility.

### 1.5 Copyright enforcement infrastructure

YouTube has the most mature copyright enforcement infrastructure of the three platforms (Content ID, manual takedown claims, strike system). A rights holder can file a takedown or claim; Content ID can match and redirect revenue or block; strikes can accumulate and threaten the channel.

**Structural point for this channel:** YouTube is the surface where a copyright claim is most likely to materialize as a concrete, trackable event. Source selection — avoiding sources with known enforcement posture — is the primary defense. This is a sustainability risk, not just an eligibility risk.

## 2. TikTok Creator Programs

### 2.1 Monetization programs

TikTok Creator rewards, Creator Marketplace, and other program-specific features. Each has eligibility requirements — account standing, originality/reused-content expectations, engagement thresholds, geographic and age requirements.

### 2.2 Structural eligibility requirements

- **Account standing and policy compliance.**
- **Originality / reused-content expectations.** Uploading content that is not original to the account, especially in patterns that suggest mass reposting or compilation of others' content, can affect eligibility.
- **Engagement and other program-specific thresholds.**
- **Geographic and age requirements.**

**Structural point for this channel:** TikTok monetization eligibility is a separate gate from reach. The channel's third-party-footage model is in the zone the reused-content expectations target. Eligibility is shaped by account history and content pattern over time.

### 2.3 Reused-content / originality rules

TikTok has meaningful policies and enforcement around reused content, unoriginal content, and compilations/aggregation. The presence of another platform's watermark or source markers can be a signal used in reuse assessment.

**Structural point for this channel:** If the channel's output reads as "reposting others' clips with minimal addition," it is in the zone TikTok's reused-content enforcement targets. The more the channel can demonstrate originality in selection, editing, overlays, and context, the more it moves away from that zone — but the model does not leave the zone entirely on the current format.

### 2.4 Sensitive-content / community guidelines

TikTok's Community Guidelines address violence, graphic content, and disturbing content. War footage — particularly imagery of death, injury, combat, destruction, and human suffering — is exactly the kind of content that can trigger removal, age-gating/mature content classification, reduced distribution, and account-level flags for repeated sensitive-content uploads.

**Structural point for this channel:** TikTok is a high-velocity, high-reach surface with aggressive and partly automated enforcement. War and news content can be both high-performing and high-risk on this surface. The platform's sensitivity enforcement and reused-content enforcement can both be triggered by the same clip.

### 2.5 Monetization eligibility and original-content expectations

TikTok's monetization features have their own eligibility requirements, which commonly include originality/reused-content expectations, account standing, engagement thresholds, and geographic/age requirements.

**Structural point for this channel:** Even when a clip is live and performs, monetization eligibility is a separate gate. The channel should plan for monetization as a later eligibility problem shaped by account history and content pattern, not as an automatic consequence of publishing.

## 3. Instagram Monetization Features

### 3.1 Monetization features

Instagram monetization features — bonuses, gifts, subscriptions, branded content, and other program-specific features. Each has eligibility criteria — original-content expectations, account standing, program-specific requirements.

### 3.2 Structural eligibility requirements

- **Original-content expectations** and account standing.
- **Program-specific requirements** — vary by feature.
- **Reach/feature eligibility** — repurposed/unoriginal content can face reduced distribution and reduced feature eligibility.

**Structural point for this channel:** Instagram is a distribution surface with a different audience. Monetization is secondary here. The channel's repurposed/unoriginal content can face reduced reach and reduced feature eligibility, but the risk is often more about distribution and features than about a binary eligibility gate.

### 3.3 Reused / repurposed content and reach

Instagram has interests in original and high-quality content, and repurposed/unoriginal content can face reduced reach/distribution penalties, account-level risk for patterns of reposting, and reduced eligibility for certain monetization or distribution features.

**Structural point for this channel:** The risk is often less about an immediate takedown and more about reduced distribution and reduced feature eligibility — but the pattern still matters, especially if the content reads as mass reposting.

### 3.4 Sensitive-content / community guidelines

Instagram's policies address violent and graphic content. War footage can be removed, age-gated/restricted, or deprioritized in distribution. This is independent of copyright.

**Structural point for this channel:** Less sensitive than war content on TikTok generally, but not zero. The channel's war clips can still be removed, age-gated, or deprioritized on Instagram.

### 3.5 Monetization on Instagram

Instagram's monetization features have their own eligibility criteria, which can include original-content expectations, account standing, and program-specific requirements. Reels is a distribution surface that can support growth and can feed monetization paths, but monetization eligibility is not automatic and is subject to platform policy.

**Structural point for this channel:** Instagram monetization is a secondary surface. The channel's Instagram strategy is distribution health and account standing first; monetization is secondary.

## 4. Cross-Platform Structural Themes

### 4.1 "Live" is not "monetizable"

A clip can be live on all three platforms and still be throttled, age-gated, or excluded from monetization eligibility on each. The eligibility gate is separate from the publishing gate.

### 4.2 Reused-content/originality is the central eligibility question for clips channels

Across platforms, the channel's third-party-footage model is the format most exposed to the originality/reused-content expectations that gate monetization. The text overlays add some transformation, but the degree of transformation matters, and the channel's overall content pattern matters.

### 4.3 Sensitivity is a separate, fast failure mode

War content's graphic/sensitive nature can remove, age-gate, or throttle clips on all three platforms, independently of copyright or originality. This affects reach, eligibility, and revenue. It's the fastest failure mode for war content.

### 4.4 Eligibility is earned over time, not granted on day one

All three platforms' monetization eligibility is earned over time, subject to content-pattern review, account history, and evolving rules. The channel starts at zero audience and zero history.

### 4.5 Rules change

Platform eligibility rules, thresholds, and enforcement evolve. A channel that is fine under today's rules can become non-eligible or non-monetizable under tomorrow's. This is a reason to build with margin and to revisit eligibility as the channel scales or the format changes.

### 4.6 Platform enforcement is not legal adjudication

Platform enforcement is contractual and policy-based, and it is often faster and less nuanced than legal analysis. A platform may remove or demonetize content that a court might ultimately treat differently. The practical lesson is not "don't rely on fair use" but "don't treat platform tolerance as proof of legal safety, and don't treat a platform takedown as proof of legal infringement — they are different systems."
